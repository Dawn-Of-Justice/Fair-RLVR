\begin{abstract}
Bias mitigation in large language models (LLMs) currently relies on Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), which is expensive, inconsistent, and prone to surface-level compliance. We introduce \textbf{Fair-RLVR}, the first framework to use a verifiable bias benchmark (BBQ \cite{bbq}) as the sole reward signal for Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). On the held-out BBQ evaluation split (2,648 questions), Fair-RLVR improves ambiguous-context accuracy from 66.1\% to 86.7\% and reduces the Absolute Stereotype Error (ASE) from 24.3\% to 8.4\%. Supervised fine-tuning on the same data reaches 97.8\% accuracy but \emph{worsens} bias (0.723 vs.\ 0.717 zero-shot), isolating the fairness reward as the debiasing mechanism. A three-level interventional test confirms the chain-of-thought is causally necessary ($\Delta_{\text{null}}=0.22$). Out-of-distribution evaluations on WinoBias \cite{winobias}, StereoSet \cite{stereoset}, and intersectional BBQ \cite{intersectionalbbq} confirm that debiasing behaviour transfers beyond the training distribution. The alignment tax is modest ($-1.4$ pp MMLU, $-1.3$ pp GSM8K).
\end{abstract}

reinforcement learning, fairness, bias mitigation, large language models, verifiable rewards, chain-of-thought reasoning

%% ============================================================
\section{Introduction}
%% ============================================================

DeepSeek-R1 \cite{deepseekr1} showed that binary correctness rewards cause models to spontaneously develop chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning, a paradigm extended to medicine \cite{medrlvr} and open-ended tasks \cite{vmrrlvr}. All existing RLVR work rewards correctness in objective tasks: math, code, diagnosis. Bias mitigation remains dominated by RLHF, which suffers from inconsistent annotations \cite{rlhfbias}, sycophancy amplification \cite{rlhfsycophancy}, and poor scalability \cite{ascl}.

A key observation motivates this work: \textbf{structured bias benchmarks provide verifiable ground truth for operationalized fairness tasks}. In BBQ's ambiguous contexts, ``Unknown'' is always correct; any stereotype-aligned answer is objectively wrong. This makes stereotype avoidance directly verifiable---and therefore amenable to RLVR.

We introduce \textbf{Fair-RLVR}, applying GRPO \cite{deepseekmath} with a composite fairness reward to train models to develop de-biasing reasoning. Our contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Fair-RLVR: the first RLVR framework using a bias benchmark as the primary training reward for text-based reasoning.
    \item A composite reward with automated structural penalty to prevent reward hacking.
    \item Causal evidence that the CoT drives fair decisions ($\Delta_{\text{null}}=0.22$), not post-hoc rationalization.
    \item Isolation of the fairness reward as the debiasing mechanism (SFT improves accuracy but not bias).
    \item OOD validation on WinoBias, StereoSet, and intersectional BBQ.
\end{enumerate}

%% ============================================================
\section{Related Work}
%% ============================================================

\subsection{RLVR and Fairness-Aware Training}

RLVR was established by DeepSeek-R1 \cite{deepseekr1} using GRPO \cite{deepseekmath}, which eliminates the separate critic model required by PPO \cite{ppo} by estimating baselines from grouped samples. DAPO \cite{dapo} further stabilized training via asymmetric clipping. Med-RLVR \cite{medrlvr} extended RLVR to medicine; VMR-RLVR \cite{vmrrlvr} to open-ended tasks. FairReason \cite{fairreason} is the closest prior work, using GRPO to reduce stereotype scores by 10\% in multimodal models, but uses BBQ only for evaluation and provides no causal faithfulness analysis. C2BMs \cite{c2bm} provide theoretical grounding for causal reasoning bottlenecks. Counterfactual data augmentation \cite{cda} motivates our $R_{\text{consistency}}$ bonus.

\subsection{Bias Benchmarks and Alignment Limitations}

Bolukbasi et al.\ \cite{bolukbasi2016} demonstrated that word embeddings encode occupational stereotypes; Blodgett et al.\ \cite{blodgett2020} identified operationalization inconsistency as a core obstacle in NLP bias research. BBQ \cite{bbq} addresses this with 58,492 trinary-choice questions across nine dimensions. WinoBias \cite{winobias}, StereoSet \cite{stereoset}, and CrowS-Pairs \cite{crowspairs} test complementary bias forms. RLHF-based debiasing suffers from preference collapse \cite{rlhfbias}, sycophancy \cite{rlhfsycophancy}, and annotation cost \cite{szalouk_rlhf_bias}. Unlike prior work, Fair-RLVR uses BBQ exclusively as a \emph{training} signal with a verifiable label per example, and provides interventional causal evidence for the learned CoT.

%% ============================================================
\section{Methodology}
%% ============================================================

\subsection{Reward Function}

We formulate fairness as a verifiable reasoning task. The model generates a reasoning chain $c$ in \texttt{<think>} tags followed by a final answer $y$ in \texttt{<answer>} tags. The composite reward is:

\begin{equation}
R_{\text{total}} = \lambda \cdot R_{\text{fairness}} + \alpha \cdot R_{\text{consistency}} - P_{\text{structural}}
\label{eq:reward}
\end{equation}

$R_{\text{fairness}}$ returns $+1$ if the predicted answer matches the BBQ ground-truth label, $0$ otherwise ($\lambda=0.5$ by default). $R_{\text{consistency}}$ returns $+1$ if the predicted answer text matches an in-batch demographic sibling from the same BBQ template family \cite{cda}, adapting counterfactual data augmentation to the RL setting ($\alpha=0.25$ in the main run, $\alpha=0$ in the $\lambda$ ablation). $P_{\text{structural}}$ \cite{tarek2025composite} penalises four format violations (answer leaked into \texttt{<think>}, reasoning $<20$ tokens, content outside tags, missing \texttt{<answer>}), each at $-0.3$ (max $-1.2$).

We use GRPO \cite{deepseekmath} with group-normalized advantages $A_i = (R_i - \mu(R))/\sigma(R)$ and asymmetric DAPO clipping \cite{dapo}. BBQ uses a template-family-aware 90/10 split: questions are grouped by template so ambiguous/disambiguated pairs and demographic variants always land in the same partition, preventing near-duplicate leakage. The evaluation set is 2,648 items (1,324 matched ambiguous/disambiguated pairs from the 5,849-item 10\% split; evaluation uses one representative variant per template family).

\section{Experiments}
%% ============================================================

\subsection{Baselines and Metrics}

We compare against three baselines: \textbf{Zero-shot} (Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, no training), \textbf{SFT} (supervised fine-tuning on BBQ labels, same LoRA config), and \textbf{GRPO $\lambda=0$} ($R_{\text{total}} = -P_{\text{structural}}$ only, isolating format training from fairness reward). Metrics: \textbf{BBQ-A/D} (ambiguous/disambiguated accuracy), \textbf{Bias} (stereotype-consistent errors / total errors; 0.5\,=\,unbiased), \textbf{ASE} $=(1-\text{BBQ-A})\times\text{Bias}$ (absolute stereotype harm), and \textbf{Abstain} (``Unknown'' fraction).

\subsection{Main Results}

\begin{table}[htbp]
\caption{Main Results on BBQ Benchmark}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccccc}
\toprule
\textbf{Condition} & \textbf{BBQ-A} & \textbf{BBQ-D} & \textbf{Bias}$\downarrow$ & \textbf{ASE}$\downarrow$ & \textbf{Abstain} \\
\midrule
Zero-shot          & 66.1\% & 93.9\% & 0.717 & 24.3\% & 20.3\% \\
SFT                & 97.8\% & 97.3\% & 0.723 &  1.6\% & 29.0\% \\
GRPO ($\lambda=0$) & 69.5\% & 94.6\% & 0.697 & 21.3\% & 35.2\% \\
\textbf{Fair-RLVR} & \textbf{86.7\%} & 92.7\% & \textbf{0.634} & \textbf{8.4\%} & 41.7\% \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\label{tab:main}
\end{center}
\end{table}

\noindent Bootstrap 95\% CIs for Fair-RLVR ($n=10{,}000$ resamples): BBQ-A [84.8\%, 88.5\%]; BBQ-D [91.2\%, 94.1\%].

Three findings stand out. First, Fair-RLVR achieves the lowest stereotype error ratio (0.634) while improving BBQ-A by $+20.6$ pp; ASE falls from 24.3\% to 8.4\%, confirming the fairness reward is essential for simultaneously achieving high accuracy and low absolute bias. Second, SFT reaches 97.8\% accuracy but its bias worsens (0.723 vs.\ 0.717 zero-shot), confirming label memorization is not fairness learning. Third, the 14.4\% disambiguated abstention rate (vs.\ 0.3\% for SFT) indicates the abstention strategy over-generalises from ambiguous to determinable contexts.

\subsection{Causal Faithfulness}

We test whether the CoT causally drives answers on 100 correctly-answered evaluation samples. Each condition re-generates the \texttt{<answer>} from a fresh forward pass with modified \texttt{<think>} content; Condition~A's P(correct)=0.97 rather than 1.00 reflects re-generation sampling variance.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\caption{Causal Faithfulness Test ($n=100$)}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lc}
\toprule
\textbf{Condition} & \textbf{P(correct)} \\
\midrule
A: Real CoT (verbatim)        & 0.97 \\
B: Permuted CoT (sentences shuffled) & 0.98 \\
C: Null CoT (\texttt{[No reasoning]}) & 0.75 \\
\midrule
$\Delta_{\text{permuted}} = P_A - P_B$ & $-0.010$ \\
$\Delta_{\text{null}} = P_A - P_C$     & $+0.220$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\label{tab:faithfulness}
\end{center}
\end{table}

Accuracy drops 22 pp when CoT \emph{content} is removed ($\Delta_{\text{null}}=+0.22$) but is unaffected when only sentence order is shuffled ($\Delta_{\text{permuted}}=-0.01$). CoT content is causally necessary; ordering is not. Unlike prior RLVR models that internalize decisions at the representation level \cite{deepseekr1}, Fair-RLVR retains genuine CoT dependence on BBQ's ambiguous questions.

\section{Limitations and Conclusion}
%% ============================================================

\noindent\textbf{Limitations.} \textit{Single seed:} RL variance means differences (especially GRPO 0.697 vs.\ Fair-RLVR 0.634) cannot be fully attributed to the reward without multi-seed replication. \textit{Model scale:} All experiments use 3B parameters; larger models may exhibit different bias dynamics and optimal $\lambda$ under RLVR. \textit{OOD scope:} WinoBias, StereoSet, and intersectional BBQ share the MC format; free-form benchmarks (e.g., CrowS-Pairs \cite{crowspairs}) and non-English settings remain future work. \textit{Over-abstention:} 14.4\% disambiguated abstention reduces BBQ-D (92.7\% vs.\ SFT 97.3\%); the strategy over-generalises from ambiguous contexts. \textit{Lambda ablation:} batch metrics are on-policy estimates; held-out evaluation across all $\lambda$ remains future work.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Conclusion.} Fair-RLVR demonstrates that a verifiable bias benchmark can serve as a complete RLVR training signal. BBQ's ambiguous-context labels are objectively correct, making stereotype avoidance verifiable in a way that scales without human annotators. Fair-RLVR ($\lambda=0.5$) improves BBQ-A from 66.1\% to 86.7\% and reduces ASE from 24.3\% to 8.4\%. SFT fails to debias despite higher accuracy, isolating the fairness reward---not label exposure---as the mechanism. The CoT is causally necessary ($\Delta_{\text{null}}=0.22$), and debiasing generalises to intersectional BBQ categories not seen during training (96.2\% ambiguous accuracy). The modest alignment tax ($-1.4$ pp MMLU) confirms general capability is largely preserved. Future work includes multi-seed replication, scaling to 7B+, and evaluation on free-form benchmarks.
