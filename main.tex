\begin{abstract}
Existing bias mitigation in LLMs relies on Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), whose subjective annotations are inconsistent and expensive, yet verifiable rewards for fairness have not been explored despite bias benchmarks offering clear ground-truth labels. We introduce \textbf{Fair-RLVR}, which uses the BBQ benchmark \cite{bbq} as an automated reward signal for Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). On the held-out BBQ split, Fair-RLVR improves ambiguous-context accuracy from 66.1\% to 86.7\%, reduces the Absolute Stereotype Error from 24.3\% to 8.4\%, and achieves the lowest stereotype error ratio of any trained model (0.634). Supervised fine-tuning on the same data reaches higher in-distribution accuracy (97.8\%) but leaves the composition of errors unchanged (Bias 0.723 vs.\ 0.717 zero-shot) and degrades out-of-distribution, recording the worst StereoSet language-modeling score and ICAT of all trained models---consistent with memorization rather than fairness learning. Fair-RLVR, by contrast, outperforms SFT on five of six out-of-distribution measures across WinoBias \cite{winobias}, StereoSet \cite{stereoset}, and intersectional BBQ \cite{intersectionalbbq}, including the highest intersectional ambiguous-context accuracy (96.2\%) on category combinations absent from training. Causal faithfulness tests confirm the chain-of-thought plays a genuine computational role ($\Delta_{\text{null}}=0.22$), and the alignment tax is modest ($-1.4$ pp MMLU, $-1.3$ pp GSM8K).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
%% ============================================================

DeepSeek-R1 \cite{deepseekr1} showed that binary correctness rewards cause models to spontaneously develop chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning. This paradigm---Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)---has been extended to medicine \cite{medrlvr} and open-ended tasks \cite{vmrrlvr}. All existing RLVR work rewards correctness in objective tasks. Meanwhile, bias mitigation remains dominated by RLHF, whose annotations are inconsistent \cite{rlhfbias}, amplify sycophancy \cite{rlhfsycophancy}, and do not scale globally \cite{ascl}.

We introduce \textbf{Fair-RLVR}, which applies RLVR to fairness alignment using the BBQ benchmark \cite{bbq} as a verifiable reward signal: in ambiguous contexts the correct answer is always ``Unknown,'' making any stereotype-aligned answer objectively wrong---directly amenable to GRPO \cite{deepseekmath} training.

Our contributions are as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item We propose Fair-RLVR, to our knowledge the first framework to use a verifiable fairness benchmark as the primary RLVR reward signal for text-based reasoning models.
    \item We design a composite reward function with an automated structural penalty to prevent reward hacking without requiring manual keyword curation.
    \item We provide causal evidence that the model's chain-of-thought genuinely drives fair decisions (sensitivity to null reasoning: $\Delta = 0.22$), rather than post-hoc rationalization.
    \item We show that supervised fine-tuning on the same data improves in-distribution accuracy but neither shifts errors away from stereotypes (SFT Bias: 0.723 vs.\ 0.717 zero-shot) nor transfers out of distribution, isolating the GRPO fairness reward as the genuine debiasing mechanism.
    \item We validate OOD generalization on WinoBias, StereoSet, and intersectional BBQ, where Fair-RLVR outperforms SFT on five of six measures, showing the learned debiasing behaviour extends beyond the BBQ training distribution.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Literature review}
%% ============================================================

\subsection{Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards}

GRPO \cite{deepseekmath} eliminates the separate critic model required by PPO \cite{ppo} by estimating baselines from grouped samples. DAPO \cite{dapo} further stabilized training via asymmetric clipping. Med-RLVR \cite{medrlvr} applied RLVR to medicine; VMR-RLVR \cite{vmrrlvr} extended it to open-ended tasks via verifiable MC reformulation.

\subsection{Bias Benchmarks and Fairness Evaluation}

Bolukbasi et al.\ \cite{bolukbasi2016} showed word embeddings encode occupational stereotypes; Blodgett et al.\ \cite{blodgett2020} identified operationalization inconsistency as a core obstacle in NLP bias research. BBQ \cite{bbq} addresses this with 58,492 trinary-choice questions across nine social dimensions, using ambiguous contexts (correct answer always ``Unknown'') and disambiguated contexts (answer determined by evidence). Prior work found models are 3.4 pp more accurate when the answer aligns with stereotypes. WinoBias \cite{winobias}, StereoSet \cite{stereoset}, and CrowS-Pairs \cite{crowspairs} test complementary bias forms and serve as OOD validators.

\subsection{Limitations of Preference-Based Alignment}

Xiao et al. \cite{rlhfbias} proved KL-divergence regularization causes ``preference collapse,'' suppressing minority viewpoints. Shapira et al. \cite{rlhfsycophancy} showed RLHF amplifies sycophancy at scale. Safety-focused approaches such as RealSafe-R1 \cite{realsafer1} and ASCL \cite{ascl} rely on human-curated reasoning trajectories, remaining annotator-dependent; GRPO \cite{deepseekmath} eliminates the separate critic required by PPO \cite{ppo}, making it more practical for fairness-specific training.

\subsection{Fairness and Reasoning}

FairReason \cite{fairreason} is the closest work to ours, using GRPO on a 1:4 bias-to-reasoning data ratio to reduce stereotype scores by 10\% while retaining 88\% of reasoning accuracy. It differs in three ways: it targets multimodal models, uses BBQ only for evaluation (not training), and does not analyze causal faithfulness. C2BMs \cite{c2bm} provide theoretical grounding by showing that causal reasoning bottlenecks improve robustness and enable ground-truth fairness interventions. Counterfactual data augmentation \cite{cda} introduced the idea of generating demographic variants of the same sentence to improve fairness; our $R_{\text{consistency}}$ reward adapts this principle to the RL setting by rewarding answer invariance across in-batch demographic siblings. Unlike all prior work, Fair-RLVR uses BBQ exclusively as a \emph{training} reward signal with a verifiable label per example, and provides interventional causal evidence that the learned CoT is computationally necessary.

\section{Methodology}
%% ============================================================

\subsection{Problem Formulation}


We formulate fairness as a verifiable reasoning task. Given an input prompt $x$ (a BBQ question with context), the model must generate a reasoning chain $c$ (within a \texttt{<think>} block) followed by a final answer $y$ (within an \texttt{<answer>} block). The task forms a Markov chain $x \rightarrow c \rightarrow y$, where the answer is conditioned on the reasoning. This creates a causal bottleneck: the model can only consistently achieve high reward by producing logically sound reasoning that leads to the correct answer.

\subsection{BBQ as Fairness Verifier}

BBQ serves as a deterministic fairness verifier: in ambiguous contexts the correct answer is always ``Unknown,'' and in disambiguated contexts the answer is determined by explicit evidence. We train on all nine social categories using a template-family-aware 90/10 split grouping by question template so that ambiguous/disambiguated pairs always land in the same partition, preventing near-duplicate leakage. This design prevents the model from memorizing specific question variants; the causal faithfulness test (Section~IV-G) provides further evidence that accuracy reflects active reasoning rather than format recall (accuracy drops 22 pp when reasoning content is removed).

\subsection{Composite Reward Function}

The reward function combines a fairness signal and an automated structural penalty:

\begin{equation}
R_{\text{total}} = \lambda \cdot R_{\text{fairness}} + \alpha \cdot R_{\text{consistency}} - P_{\text{structural}}
\label{eq:reward}
\end{equation}

\noindent where:

\begin{itemize}
    \item $R_{\text{fairness}}$ compares the model's answer against the BBQ ground-truth label: $+1$ for a match, $0$ for a mismatch.
    \item $\lambda$ controls the weight of the fairness signal (default: $\lambda = 0.5$). Maximum reward at $\alpha = 0$ is $\lambda = 0.5$.
    \item $R_{\text{consistency}}$ is a counterfactual-consistency bonus (adapted from Szalouk et al.\ \cite{szalouk_rlhf_bias} CDA): $+1.0$ if the model's predicted answer text matches an in-batch demographic variant from the same BBQ template family, $0$ otherwise. $\alpha = 0.25$ for the main experiment; $\alpha = 0$ in the lambda ablation study.
    \item $P_{\text{structural}}$ is a rule-based penalty inspired by recent work on composite verifiable rewards \cite{tarek2025composite}. It detects four structural violations: (1) the answer appearing inside the \texttt{<think>} block (answer leaking), (2) an empty or trivially short reasoning chain ($<20$ tokens), (3) reasoning content appearing outside the designated tags, and (4) a missing \texttt{<answer>} tag. Each violation incurs a fixed penalty of $0.3$, for a maximum total penalty of $1.2$.
\end{itemize}


\subsection{Training with GRPO}

We use Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) \cite{deepseekmath} for training. For each input prompt, $G = 4$ candidate outputs are sampled. The reward $R_i$ is computed for each output, and advantages are normalized within the group:

\begin{equation}
A_i = \frac{R_i - \mu(R)}{\sigma(R)}
\label{eq:advantage}
\end{equation}

\noindent where $\mu(R)$ and $\sigma(R)$ are the mean and standard deviation of rewards within the group. The policy is updated using a clipped surrogate objective with KL-divergence regularization to prevent excessive drift from the base model. Following DAPO \cite{dapo}, we employ asymmetric clipping ratios ($\epsilon_{\text{high}} = 0.28$, $\epsilon_{\text{low}} = 0.20$) to maintain exploration and prevent entropy collapse.

\section{Experiments}
%% ============================================================

\subsection{Baselines}

We compare Fair-RLVR against three baselines to isolate the contribution of each component:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Zero-shot:} Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct with no additional training, evaluated directly on BBQ.
    \item \textbf{SFT:} The same model fine-tuned with supervised learning on BBQ question-answer pairs, without reinforcement learning or chain-of-thought reasoning.
    \item \textbf{GRPO ($\lambda=0$):} The model trained with GRPO using only the structural penalty ($R_{\text{total}} = -P_{\text{structural}}$), without the fairness reward signal. This isolates whether the GRPO optimization process alone improves fairness, independent of the fairness reward.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}

We report: \textbf{BBQ-A} (ambiguous-context accuracy; primary fairness metric), \textbf{BBQ-D} (disambiguated-context accuracy), \textbf{Bias} (stereotype-consistent errors / total errors; 0.5 = unbiased, lower = fewer stereotype-aligned errors), \textbf{ASE} (Absolute Stereotype Error $= (1-\text{BBQ-A})\times\text{Bias}$; measures absolute stereotype harm independent of the error ratio), and \textbf{Abstention Rate} (fraction of responses whose answer text is an explicit ``Unknown''-form string, matched against per-question \texttt{unknown\_label} indices rather than a fixed position).

\noindent \textit{Bias}$\downarrow$ = stereotype-consistent errors / total errors (0.5 = unbiased). \textit{ASE}$\downarrow$ = $(1 - \text{BBQ-A}) \times \text{Bias}$. Bootstrap 95\% CIs for Fair-RLVR (10,000 resamples, $n=2{,}648$): BBQ-A [84.8\%, 88.5\%]; BBQ-D [91.2\%, 94.1\%].

Three findings stand out. First, Fair-RLVR achieves the lowest stereotype error ratio (0.634) of all trained models, outperforming both GRPO (0.697) and SFT (0.723), while simultaneously improving ambiguous-context accuracy by +20.6 pp over zero-shot. The ASE column shows Fair-RLVR at 8.4\% versus GRPO at 21.3\%, demonstrating that the explicit fairness reward is essential for simultaneously achieving high accuracy and low absolute bias.

Second, SFT achieves 97.8\% accuracy and, as a direct arithmetic consequence ($\text{ASE} = (1-\text{BBQ-A})\times\text{Bias}$), the lowest ASE (1.6\%). However, this low ASE reflects \emph{error suppression}, not \emph{error debiasing}: the few errors SFT does make are slightly \emph{more} stereotype-aligned than zero-shot (Bias 0.723 vs.\ 0.717), meaning SFT has memorized BBQ's label distribution without altering the stereotype-driven failure mode it falls back on when memorization misses. The Bias ratio is the metric that isolates this failure mode, and on it only Fair-RLVR moves the needle (0.717 $\to$ 0.634). The OOD results (Table~\ref{tab:ood}) confirm this interpretation: outside the memorized distribution, SFT records the worst StereoSet LMS (0.911) and ICAT (0.536) of all trained models and trails Fair-RLVR on intersectional BBQ in both contexts. In-distribution ASE is therefore not a reliable proxy for fairness under distribution shift, whereas Fair-RLVR's gains persist there.

Third, Fair-RLVR's elevated abstention rate (41.7\% overall; 68.9\% on ambiguous, 14.4\% on disambiguated) reflects the learned strategy of withholding judgment when the evidence is insufficient---precisely the behaviour BBQ defines as correct in ambiguous contexts. The 14.4\% disambiguated abstention represents partial over-abstention where the model withholds commitment on questions with determinable answers (SFT's corresponding rate is 0.3\%); we view this as epistemically conservative failure---declining to answer---rather than stereotype-aligned failure, and quantify the resulting accuracy cost (92.7\% vs.\ 97.3\% BBQ-D) in Section~V.

\subsection{Causal Faithfulness}

We perform a three-level interventional test on 100 correctly-answered evaluation samples. Each condition prepends modified \texttt{<think>} content and re-generates the answer (fresh forward pass, so P(correct$\mid$real CoT)\,=\,0.97 due to sampling variance, not a floor on first-pass accuracy):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Condition A (real CoT):} The model's original reasoning chain is provided verbatim; the model re-generates the answer.
    \item \textbf{Condition B (permuted CoT):} Sentences within the \texttt{<think>} block are randomly shuffled; this preserves lexical content but destroys logical ordering.
    \item \textbf{Condition C (null CoT):} The \texttt{<think>} block is replaced with \texttt{[No reasoning provided]}; this removes all reasoning content.
\end{itemize}

\begin{table}[htbp]
\caption{Causal Faithfulness Test}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lc}
\toprule
\textbf{Metric} & \textbf{Value} \\
\midrule
P(correct $\mid$ real CoT) & 0.97 \\
P(correct $\mid$ permuted CoT) & 0.98 \\
P(correct $\mid$ null CoT) & 0.75 \\
Sensitivity (permuted) $= P_A - P_B$ & $-0.010$ \\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

\end{center}
\end{table}

Accuracy drops 22 percentage points when CoT content is removed ($\Delta_{\text{null}} = +0.22$) but is unaffected when only sentence order is shuffled ($\Delta_{\text{permuted}} = -0.01$). The permuted condition serves as a control for surface-format effects: it perturbs the \texttt{<think>} block just as invasively as the null condition while preserving content, and accuracy is unchanged---indicating the null-condition drop is attributable to loss of reasoning content rather than mere format disruption. This shows the \textit{content} of reasoning is causally necessary while its \textit{ordering} is not; the model extracts evidence from individual sentences rather than relying on sequential structure. Unlike prior RLVR models that internalize decision rules at the representation level \cite{deepseekr1}, Fair-RLVR retains genuine CoT dependence, suggesting BBQ's ambiguous questions require active reasoning.

\subsection{Out-of-Distribution Generalization}

%% ============================================================
\section{Limitations}
%% ============================================================

\noindent\textbf{OOD and benchmark scope.} OOD evaluations on WinoBias, StereoSet, and intersectional BBQ (Table~\ref{tab:ood}) confirm transfer beyond training, but these benchmarks share structural similarities with BBQ (MC format, English). Free-form generation benchmarks (e.g., CrowS-Pairs \cite{crowspairs}) and global cultural contexts (BharatBBQ) remain untested.

\noindent\textbf{Model scale.} Experiments use a single model size (3B parameters). Larger models (7B+) may benefit more from verifiable reward signals but also exhibit stronger pre-training biases, and whether $\lambda=0.5$ remains optimal at scale is an open question.

\noindent\textbf{Single seed.} All experiments use seed 42; RL training variance means small margins (e.g., GRPO bias 0.697 vs.\ Fair-RLVR 0.634) should be interpreted cautiously. Multiple seeds would strengthen causal claims.

\noindent\textbf{Over-abstention on disambiguated questions.} Fair-RLVR abstains (outputs ``Unknown'') on 14.4\% of disambiguated BBQ questions where a definitive answer is available, compared to 0.3\% for SFT. This over-abstention reduces disambiguated-context accuracy (92.7\% vs.\ SFT 97.3\%) and suggests the abstention strategy learned from ambiguous contexts partially over-generalises to determinable contexts. We note these are epistemically conservative errors (declining to commit) rather than stereotype-aligned ones; nonetheless, calibrating abstention more precisely is a clear direction for improvement.

\noindent\textbf{Uneven per-category improvement.} Fair-RLVR increases the stereotype error \emph{ratio} in SES, Nationality, and Race/Ethnicity relative to zero-shot---an artifact of shrinking error denominators---though ASE, the absolute-harm measure, decreases across all nine categories (see Section~IV-D analysis).


%% ============================================================
\section{Conclusion}
%% ============================================================

This paper introduces Fair-RLVR, a framework that applies Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards to fairness alignment in language models. By using the BBQ benchmark as a deterministic fairness verifier, we replace subjective human feedback with an automated, reproducible training signal.

Fair-RLVR ($\lambda=0.5$) improves ambiguous-context accuracy from 66.1\% to 86.7\%, reduces ASE from 24.3\% to 8.4\%, and is the only trained model to shift the composition of errors away from stereotypes (Bias 0.717 $\to$ 0.634), while SFT---despite higher in-distribution accuracy---leaves the stereotype error ratio unchanged and degrades out of distribution, trailing Fair-RLVR on five of six OOD measures. Causal faithfulness analysis ($\Delta_{\text{null}} = 0.22$) confirms the chain-of-thought plays a genuine computational role, and Fair-RLVR achieves the highest intersectional BBQ ambiguous accuracy (96.2\%) on demographic combinations never seen in training.

The central insight is that structured bias benchmarks can provide the verifiable labels needed for RLVR-based fairness training---opening a path toward scalable, automated bias reduction without human annotators, and producing debiasing behaviour that, unlike supervised fine-tuning, survives distribution shift. Future work includes scaling to larger models and evaluating on culturally diverse benchmarks such as BharatBBQ.
